What margin call and stop out actually mean

A margin call is your broker's warning that your account equity is dangerously low — close to the margin required to keep positions open. A stop out is what happens next: the broker closes your trades

What a margin call is

A margin call happens when your account equity drops close to the margin required to keep your open positions alive. It's a warning — not a liquidation, not yet. Your broker is telling you: "Add more money, or close some trades, or we'll do it for you."

Different brokers set different thresholds. Some will notify you at 100% margin level (equity equals used margin). Others wait until 80%. The number varies, but the message is the same: you're running out of room.

What stop out means

If you don't act — or if the market moves too fast for you to react — the broker will trigger a stop out. This is an automatic closure of your positions, usually when your margin level falls to 50% or lower.

The broker isn't punishing you. It's protecting itself. Leverage means you're trading with borrowed money. If your equity falls below what you owe, the broker is on the hook. Stop out is the circuit breaker that prevents that.

A Filipino example

Let's say you deposit ₱10,000 into a broker account. You open a 0.5-lot position on USD/PHP at 1:100 leverage. The required margin is around ₱2,800. Your equity is ₱10,000, so your margin level is roughly 357%.

Now the trade moves against you. You're down ₱7,500. Your equity is now ₱2,500. Your margin level has dropped to about 89%. You get a margin call warning.

You ignore it. Price keeps moving. Your equity hits ₱1,400. Margin level: 50%. The broker closes your position automatically. You're left with ₱1,400 in your account — a realized loss of ₱8,600.

You didn't "lose to the broker." You lost to position size and leverage you couldn't handle.

The hard truth

If you've ever been margin-called or stopped out, the mistake wasn't in that moment. It was three decisions earlier:

  • You opened a position too large for your account.
  • You didn't set a stop loss, or set it too wide.
  • You added to a losing trade instead of cutting it.

Margin call is the symptom. Overleveraging is the disease.

What to do instead

  • Never use more than 1–2% of your account equity as risk per trade.
  • Always know your margin level before you open a position.
  • Set a stop loss that gets you out long before your broker has to.

If your broker ever has to close a trade for you, you've already failed the test.
