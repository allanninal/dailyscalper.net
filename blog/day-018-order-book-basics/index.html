What is the order book? (And why you can't see it)

In stock trading, the order book is public: you see every buy and sell order waiting at each price. In forex, there is no central order book. The market is over-the-counter, decentralized. Some broker

If you've ever traded stocks on the Philippine Stock Exchange or watched a US equity platform, you've probably seen the order book — a live list of all the buy orders (bids) and sell orders (asks) waiting at each price level. It's public. Everyone sees the same thing.

Forex is different.

Forex has no central order book

Forex is an over-the-counter (OTC) market. That means there's no single exchange where all orders are routed and displayed. Instead, trading happens through a decentralized network of banks, brokers, hedge funds, and market makers — all quoting prices to each other, often through private relationships.

There is no "master list" of pending orders. No ticker tape. No unified depth chart that everyone can see.

What your broker shows you

Some brokers — especially those offering ECN (Electronic Communication Network) accounts — do provide what's called Level II data or market depth. This is a window into the orders sitting on their liquidity providers' books.

You might see something like this:

  • 56.52 — 2.5 million USD to sell
  • 56.50 — 4.0 million USD to buy
  • 56.48 — 1.8 million USD to buy

That's useful. But it's not the whole market. It's a slice — one broker's view of one network's liquidity at one moment.

Why it matters for scalpers

If you're trying to scalp 5–15 pips on a pair like USD/PHP, knowing where clusters of buy or sell orders sit can help you anticipate where price might stall, bounce, or break through.

Let's say you see a thick wall of buy orders at 56.50. That suggests institutional players or algo systems are willing to defend that level. Price may pause there. If it breaks, those orders get filled and the next support level comes into play.

It's not a guarantee. It's a hint — and in a market with no public order book, hints matter.

A Manila example

Imagine you're a trader in Makati watching USD/PHP during the 4 p.m. BSP fix window. Your broker's Level II shows a large sell order at 56.55. That might be a corporate client converting dollar receivables into pesos for payroll. If that order absorbs all the buy-side momentum, price stalls. If buyers push through it, you get a breakout.

You didn't know it would happen. But you saw the setup.

The limitation

Remember: what you see is not exhaustive. A big bank in Singapore might have a huge order sitting elsewhere that never shows up on your screen. Price can slice through your broker's "support" like it wasn't there — because globally, it wasn't.

Treat Level II as context, not prophecy.
