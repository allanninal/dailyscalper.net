How bid, ask, and spread work together

The quote you see

Open any forex platform and you'll see a two-sided price. Let's use EUR/USD as the example:

1.0850 / 1.0852

The left number is the bid — the price at which the market will buy euros from you (or, if you're short, the price you pay to close).

The right number is the ask — the price at which the market will sell euros to you (or, if you're long, the price you pay to open).

The gap between them — in this case 2 pips — is the spread. It's the broker's cut, the market's transaction cost, and your first obstacle.

What happens when you buy

You believe the euro will strengthen. You click buy. Your platform executes at the ask: 1.0852.

Now look at your open position. Most platforms will show an unrealised profit/loss immediately. What does it say?

–2 pips.

You haven't held the trade for even one second. The price hasn't moved. But you're already down.

Why you start negative

Because if you closed that position right now, you'd exit at the bid: 1.0850. You bought at 1.0852. The difference is the spread, and you paid it the instant you entered.

This is not a bug. It's not your broker cheating you. It's the structure of the market. The spread is the cost of doing business, and it's deducted up front as an unrealised loss.

What it takes to break even

For your trade to show zero profit or loss, the bid must rise to match your entry price.

You entered at 1.0852 (the ask). Break-even happens when the bid reaches 1.0852. At that point, the quote might look like 1.0852 / 1.0854 — the market has moved 2 pips in your favor, and you've clawed back the spread.

Only after that do you start making money.

A Filipino scenario

Imagine you're a Makati-based trader. You open a 0.10-lot EUR/USD long at 1.0852. Your position size means each pip is worth about ₱50.

The moment you enter, you're down ₱100. The price hasn't moved. You just paid the spread.

If EUR/USD rallies to 1.0860 (8 pips), your unrealised profit is not ₱400. It's ₱300 — because the first 2 pips went to covering the spread.

Why this matters

Most beginners don't realize the spread is a cost per trade, not a one-time account fee. If you open and close ten trades in a day, you pay the spread ten times. Scalpers and high-frequency traders live or die by spread size.

It also means that tight spreads matter more the shorter your holding period. If you're swing trading over days or weeks, a 2-pip spread is noise. If you're trying to scalp 5-pip moves, that same spread just ate 40% of your target.

The honest truth

You are not starting from zero when you enter a trade. You are starting from negative. The market does not owe you a win just because you clicked a button. Your first job is to recover what you paid to get in.
